# Strategy and cadence

## The archive is finite; the habit is not

An archive of a few dozen hour-long episodes yields well over a hundred clips at 3–5 per
video — several months of daily Shorts without recording anything new. That buys time,
and time is what it is for. It does not replace the recording habit.

The durable change is upstream: **record with clips in mind.** Ask guests questions whose
answers are self-contained 40-second stories with a beginning and a payoff. A podcast
recorded that way yields 5–6 usable clips instead of 2–3, permanently.

Note when a great clip was found by accident. That is a question format worth repeating
deliberately.

## Weekly loop (~45 minutes of the creator's time)

1. `find-candidates.js --all` — mechanical, seconds
2. Read the candidates, offer 8–10 with written hooks — this is the work
3. Creator picks 5
4. `render-short.js` ×5 — a few minutes
5. Creator reviews, approves, **uploads themselves**

The creator's time goes entirely into judgment: picking and approving. Everything else
is automated. Resist the urge to automate step 2 — a scored list is not a
recommendation, and handing over unread candidates transfers work rather than removing it.

## Content mix

Set it from the channel's own medians (see `channel-data.md`), not from intuition or
general advice. Re-measure every 20–30 uploads; audiences shift.

A pipeline that produces more of an underperforming category faster has made things
worse, not better. If the data shows a mismatch between effort and return, fixing the
mix comes before any tooling improvement.

## Measurement

One number: **median views of the next 20 Shorts versus the previous median.**

Set the target before publishing, not after. If the median has not moved after 20
uploads, the selection rules are wrong — change the rules, not the tools. Tooling
changes feel productive and almost never move this number.

Ignore total views (grows with upload count regardless of quality) and best-clip views
(noise).

## Sequencing

Order matters more than it seems:

1. **One clip, end to end, reviewed by the creator.** Establishes the layout, caption
   style, and hook voice. Everything downstream inherits these.
2. **Five clips from one video.** Tests whether the process holds without the first
   clip's beginner's luck.
3. **Batch across the archive.** Only after the medians confirm the selection rules work.
4. **Language expansion** (dubbing to a second language) — last, and only for clips
   already proven in the original language. Dubbing an unproven clip pays twice for the
   same guess.

Skipping to step 3 produces a large volume of clips in a style nobody has validated.

## What not to build

**Automated uploading.** The value is in the judgment; removing the creator from the
loop removes the only part that is scarce. Also it is their name on the channel.

**AI-generated video from scratch.** Stock footage and synthetic narration compete with
thousands of identical channels and attract authenticity enforcement. An archive of
original footage is a real moat. Do not trade it for volume.

**Face tracking / auto-reframe**, until the simple layout demonstrably limits results.
It is the most requested feature and the least likely to move the median.
